The future is being designed by a small number of people: those building the technologies, systems and institutions everyone else will live inside. Most people experience change as something that happens to them, not something they take part in.

Why the gap is growing

The skills people were taught to prove their worth are being absorbed.

It shows first at the start of careers, where knowledge learned from books and courses is most of what a person brings.

55%

of Indian IT firms saw entry-level employment fall after adopting AI. At mid level, only 25% did.

ICRIER, AI and Jobs: This Time is No Different. 651 IT firms in 10 Indian cities, surveyed Nov 2025 to Jan 2026. Supported by OpenAI.

Technology is absorbing the very skills people spent years building, above all the knowledge that once set them apart. Meanwhile, the way people learn, work and grow was designed for a slower, more predictable world. People are being trained for a test that is no longer being set.

See the evidence
  • Employers expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030, and 63% name skill gaps as the biggest barrier to transforming their business.World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025
  • Of every 100 workers, 59 will need training by 2030, and 11 of those are not expected to get it.World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025
  • Across 31 countries, adult literacy has stagnated or declined over the past decade in most places, including among university graduates.OECD Survey of Adult Skills, December 2024
  • Only 4% of Indian IT firms have trained more than half their workforce in AI-related skills.ICRIER, 2026
  • Net hiring by Indian IT companies fell from about 6 lakh in FY22 to about 1.4 lakh in FY26.Xpheno, cited by Nomura, August 2026
  • Not every entry-level job is affected. Overall fresher hiring in India rose 17% year on year in February 2026, led by non-IT sectors. The squeeze is concentrated in AI-exposed work.Naukri JobSpeak, February 2026

See how the lab works

Research, innovation and transformation, run as one engine.

What the lab learns becomes what it builds, and what it builds changes how people live.

01

Research

Learns what is changing, in humans, in society, in technology and in the future.

For example: what happens to a person's judgment when AI drafts the first answer?

02

Innovation

Builds a response, with new ideas, models, tools and possibilities grounded in what research found.

For example: a format for practising judgment rather than recall.

03

Transformation

Changes how people live, for individuals and for groups.

For example: running it with real people in their real setting, and checking it is still in use months later.

Why all three

Research without innovation stays on paper, innovation without research solves the wrong problem, and neither changes anything without transformation. Together they make change both right and real.

Many organisations do one part well. Research bodies understand what is changing, training and education providers bring change to people at scale, and builders create new tools. BE Human Labs works alongside all of them. Its difference is running all three as one engine.

Talk to us What it transforms

Four dimensions, developed together.

They move a person from Forward Thinker to Active Shaper.

Four strands make the knot.

Awareness

Who am I, and how am I connected to the people and the world around me?

Awareness lets a person see what is changing and imagine what could be.

Capability

What can I do, and how can I do it better?

Transforming your skills gives you the means to act, including knowing when to use tools like AI and when not to rely on them.

Connection

Who can I do this with?

Drawing on collective power lets people create change no one could alone.

Contribution

What will I actually change, and will I see it through?

Contribution carries action through to a real outcome. Standard skills lists describe traits; this asks whether change actually happened.

Forward Thinker and Active Shaper

A Forward Thinker looks beyond the present, questions assumptions, understands emerging possibilities and imagines what could be.

An Active Shaper takes that understanding into action by experimenting, creating, collaborating and contributing. A person draws on all four dimensions every time they shape something, which is why they grow together rather than in order.

What growth might look like
  • Awareness: naming your strengths, patterns and blind spots, and noticing how your choices affect others.
  • Capability: learning new things faster, and using tools to do more, while knowing when not to rely on them.
  • Connection: seeking out people who think differently, and bringing a group to act together.
  • Contribution: starting things and finishing them, and pointing to a change that exists because of you.

These are illustrative. Defining the real signs, starting with Contribution, is the lab's first research question.

Four offerings, each doing something the others can't.

People guide it

Mentors, practitioners and a community of shapers who walk alongside change, bringing judgment and lived experience no tool can supply.

Products scale it

Tools, frameworks and experiences that put transformation in people's hands, reaching more people than any guide could alone.

Programs structure it

Structured journeys that give change a path and a pace, taking individuals and groups from intent to change.

Systems sustain it

Redesigned environments and institutions, because what people learn sticks only when the environment around them supports using it.

How the offerings grow and combine

BE Human Labs is designed so that the people it develops become the people it offers. Today's participants are meant to become tomorrow's mentors and practitioners.

The offerings also work together: a program uses a product, and people help redesign systems. Most transformations draw on more than one.

Why systems matter
  • A meta-analysis of 89 studies found the work environment shapes whether training is applied on the job, especially for open skills such as leadership and interpersonal skills.Blume, Ford, Baldwin and Huang, Journal of Management, 2010
  • Peer, supervisor and organisational support each independently predict whether training lasts. Peer support explained the most.Hughes, Zajac, Woods and Salas, Human Factors, 2020
