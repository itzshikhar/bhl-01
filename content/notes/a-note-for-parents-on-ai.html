Most parents first hear about AI and homework the same way: a child finishes a difficult assignment suspiciously fast, or a relative forwards a news story late at night. Either way, the worry arrives before the explanation. Schools that speak first, calmly and clearly, turn that worry into partnership.

This note is written for school leaders to adapt and send home. The first part explains why it matters. The note itself follows, ready to use.

The phone is already in the home, and AI is already on the phone. What is missing is a shared understanding between school and family of how children should use it. Parents are the people best placed to notice when a child is outsourcing their thinking, if someone tells them what to look for.

The note

Ready to send.

Dear parents,

You may have noticed your child using AI tools on a phone or computer, perhaps for homework. Many of you have asked us what this means and what you should do. This note is our answer.

What is changing.

AI tools can now answer questions, solve problems and write essays in seconds, in English and in many Indian languages. Several are now free in India. Used well, they can help a child understand something they are stuck on. Used badly, they can do the work for them, and the child learns nothing, even though the homework looks perfect.

What we are doing at school.

  • Teaching students when AI helps their learning and when it replaces it.
  • Changing some homework so it asks children to show their thinking, not only their answers.
  • Checking understanding in class, through short conversations and explanations.

An age-by-age guide.

AgeWhat healthy use looks likeA simple house rule
Classes 1 to 5Exploring AI together with a parent, asking it questions out of curiosity.Only with a grown-up sitting beside you.
Classes 6 to 8Asking AI to explain something after trying it yourself first.Try first, then ask. Tell us when you used it.
Classes 9 to 12Using AI to check, quiz and challenge your own work.Use it to understand, never to copy. Be able to explain everything you submit.

Five ways you can help at home.

  1. Ask your child to explain."Tell me how you solved this." If they can explain it, they learned it.
  2. Ask about the struggle."What was hard today?" Struggle is part of learning. Praise effort, not only marks.
  3. Agree simple rules.Try it yourself first. Use AI to understand, not to copy. Tell the truth about when you used it.
  4. Explore it together.Ask an AI tool a question as a family, then talk about whether the answer was right.
  5. Keep time for people.Meals, conversations and play matter more now, not less.

Signs your child may be outsourcing their thinking

  • Homework is finished very quickly but they cannot explain it.
  • Written work suddenly sounds unlike them.
  • They reach for the phone before they have tried anything.

Three questions for the dinner table.

  • What question did you ask today?
  • What did you figure out on your own?
  • What is something AI can't do that you can?

If you have questions or worries, please speak to your child's class teacher. We would rather hear them early.

With warm regards,
[Principal's name]
[School name]

Why this matters to the lab

Change lasts when families carry it.

At BE Human Labs, we think of transformation as something that has to reach beyond the classroom to stick. A child spends far more waking hours at home than at school. When parents ask "how did you work that out?" instead of "did you finish?", the school's work continues at the dinner table. That is connection, one of our four dimensions, doing its job.
