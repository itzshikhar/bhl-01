At the end of the school day in many Japanese schools, there are no cleaners. The children sweep the classrooms, wipe the corridors and clean the toilets, in small rotating teams. At lunch, they serve each other. Ask a Japanese teacher why, and they rarely mention hygiene or saving money. They talk about responsibility, about noticing what others need, about a class learning to work as one.

Nobody calls it a values lesson. It is simply how the day works. That is precisely why it lasts.

For most of history, school was where knowledge lived. The teacher and the textbook held what children could not find elsewhere. That reason for school has weakened. A child with a phone can reach more explanation than any library their grandparents knew. Yet the case for school has not weakened. It has moved.

1 in 5

adolescents aged 13 to 17 worldwide report feeling lonely, the highest rate of any age group. One in four is socially isolated.

WHO Commission on Social Connection, June 2025
15 < 10

Across cities worldwide, 15-year-olds report lower curiosity and creativity than 10-year-olds.

OECD Survey of Social and Emotional Skills, 2021

These two findings point the same way. Young people are more connected to information and less connected to each other than any generation before them. And the capacities they arrive with, curiosity and creativity, fade during the years they spend in school. A school that sees itself as a place to deliver content will not fix either. A school that sees itself as a place to practise being human can.

Part one

What only a school has.

An AI tutor can explain photosynthesis well. It cannot give a child any of the four things below. These are a school's real assets, and most schools undervalue all of them.

The room

People you did not choose.

Why it matters
Children learn to share, wait and disagree with people unlike them. Algorithms show us people like ourselves.
What it builds
Connection: trust across difference.

Time

Years together, not sessions.

Why it matters
Relationships that last long enough to go wrong and be put right.
What it builds
Repair, loyalty, patience.

Consequence

What you do affects others.

Why it matters
A missed duty means someone else's lunch is late. Responsibility becomes real.
What it builds
Contribution: owning outcomes.

Witness

Adults who notice.

Why it matters
A teacher sees a change in a child before the child says anything.
What it builds
Awareness: being known helps you know yourself.

Part two

Practised, not taught.

You cannot teach responsibility in a lecture, any more than you can teach swimming on a whiteboard. These capacities grow through repeated practice in real situations. Many schools already have the raw material: assembly, houses, prefects, sports days, service weeks. The question is whether these are treated as the real work or as breaks from it.

Most schools invest in the top row. The bottom-right square is where character is actually built.

NEP 2020 points the same way. It asks schools to develop not only cognitive capacities but social, ethical and emotional ones, and to move from content-heavy teaching to experiential learning. The policy gives permission. The timetable has to follow.

Part three

Three spheres, one school day.

At BE Human Labs, we see change happening in three spheres at once: the individual, society and humanity. Every Active Shaper widens the circle, changing their own path, how their community works and where humanity heads. A school day can touch all three.

The individual
A daily job others depend on. A weekly reflection on what went well and what did not.
Society
Circles where classmates resolve disagreements. Older students looking out for younger ones. A project that changes something outside the gate.
Humanity
Conversations about the big questions, from climate to technology, and what each student will do about them.

Have a practice in your school that builds connection or contribution every day? We are collecting them from Indian schools to share with the network.
