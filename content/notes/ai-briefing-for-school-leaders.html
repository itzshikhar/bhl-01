On 4 November 2025, a paid AI assistant became free for a year to anyone in India who signed up. Within weeks, other companies had made similar offers to students and mobile subscribers. No school board met to decide whether children should have access. They simply did, on the phones already in their pockets.

School leaders do not need to follow every AI announcement. They need to know which changes reach the classroom, the staff room and the parents' WhatsApp group. This briefing picks those out, says what each means for a school, and suggests what to do this term.

Part one

Five shifts that reached schools.

1. AI became free

Access is no longer the barrier.

What changed
ChatGPT Go became free in India for 12 months from November 2025. Google offered students a free year of Gemini, and Airtel bundled Perplexity Pro for its subscribers.
What it means for you
Assume every student with a phone can use a capable AI assistant, in English and many Indian languages, by voice or by photo.

2. Policy arrived

AI is now part of the curriculum.

What changed
The Ministry of Education announced an AI and computational thinking curriculum from Class 3 in all schools, starting in 2026-27, framed by CBSE and NCERT under NCF-SE 2023.
What it means for you
Teachers will be asked to teach about AI before most have been taught how to use it well. Training cannot wait for the textbook.

3. Children's data law got teeth

Consent is now the school's job.

What changed
The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025. Anyone handling data of under-18s will need verifiable parental consent, with no behavioural tracking or targeted advertising. Most duties apply within 18 months.
What it means for you
Every tool your school uses with children is now a compliance question. Start an inventory of what you use and what it collects.

4. The evidence sharpened

How AI is used decides whether it helps.

What changed
In a study of nearly 1,000 high school students, open AI access raised practice scores by 48% but cut exam scores by 17% once the AI was taken away. A tutor version with teacher-designed guardrails avoided the harm.
What it means for you
The question is not whether students use AI. It is whether the way they use it makes them think more or less.

5. The job market moved

The first rung is changing.

What changed
In an ICRIER survey of around 650 IT firms, 55% reported a fall in entry-level hiring after adopting AI, and 63% now prefer candidates with hybrid skills.
What it means for you
Careers guidance built on streams alone is out of date. Students need judgment and the ability to work alongside AI before they graduate.

A thread runs through all five: access to AI is now universal, so what separates students is how they use it.

Part two

Ignore, watch, act.

Not every headline deserves a staff meeting. Here is how we would sort this quarter's noise.

Safely ignore

Detectors and doom.

AI-writing detectors are unreliable and wrongly flag non-native English writers. Claims that AI will replace teachers. Pressure to buy something this term.

Watch

Rules and rollouts.

CBSE and NCERT material for the Class 3 to 8 AI curriculum. Final guidance on parental consent under the DPDP Rules. New AI features inside the apps your school already uses.

Act now

Guide, redesign, tell.

A one-page student AI guide. One redesigned homework task per department. A short note to parents. Each takes weeks, not months.

Want a ready-made student AI guide to adapt? We share a one-page template with schools that ask, written in plain language and tested with students.

Part three

A plan for this term.

Each step leaves something visible behind, so you can show staff and parents what has changed.

  1. Weeks 1 to 2

    Take stock.

    List every digital tool used with students and what data it collects. Ask five teachers and five students how they use AI today. You will learn more from these ten conversations than from any report.

  2. Weeks 3 to 6

    Write the guide.

    Agree a one-page AI guide for students, with clear examples of use that helps learning and use that replaces it. Share it in assembly and in every classroom.

  3. Weeks 7 to 10

    Redesign one task each.

    Every department rewrites one homework or assessment task so the thinking happens where a teacher can see it: in class, aloud, or in drafts.

  4. End of term

    Tell parents.

    Send a short note explaining what the school is doing and why, and invite questions. Parents want to hear from the school before they hear from the news.
