Ask teachers what wears them down and few say the teaching. They say the marking, the records, the lesson plans, the circulars, the parent messages and the worksheets that need three versions for three kinds of learner. This is the work around teaching. It is where AI can help most, soonest, and with the least risk to students.

A 2025 Gallup survey of 2,232 US public school teachers found that those who use AI at least weekly save an estimated 5.9 hours a week. Across a school year, that is about six weeks of working time. Most said the quality of their work improved too, not just the speed. No comparable figure exists yet for Indian teachers, who spend even more of their week on work around teaching.

Part one

Where a teacher's week goes.

In India, the problem is sharper than in most countries. A NUEPA study of government school teachers in five states found that only 19.1% of working hours went to actual teaching. The rest was split across other duties.

Source: NUEPA, Involvement of Teachers in Non-teaching Activities, 2018. Government school teachers in five states.

AI cannot take election duty off a teacher's plate. But a large share of the core non-teaching work, such as planning, preparing materials, drafting feedback and writing to parents, is exactly what today's AI tools do well.

Part two

What AI drafts, what the teacher decides.

The rule is simple: AI drafts, the teacher decides. Here is how that splits across the most common tasks.

TaskAI draftsTeacher decides
Differentiated materialThree versions of one worksheet: support, core, challenge.Which child gets which, and whether the challenge is right.
Practice questionsTwenty varied questions on a topic.The eight worth using, checked for errors.
FeedbackComments on the five most common mistakes in a set of answers.The personal note each student needs.
Parent messagesA clear notice, translated into Hindi or another home language.Tone, accuracy and whether it should be a call instead.
Lesson startersFive hooks, analogies or discussion questions.The one that fits this class today.
RubricsA first draft of a four-point rubric.What "good" really means in this school.

A prompt to try today

"I teach Class 7 science in a CBSE school in India. Here is my worksheet on the water cycle. Make three versions: one for students who need support, one for most of the class, and one with an extra challenge. Keep the same learning goal and use Indian examples."

Part three

Four guardrails.

Data

No names in public tools.

Never paste student names, marks or personal details into a public AI tool. India's DPDP Rules 2025 make children's data a serious legal responsibility for schools.

Accuracy

Check everything.

AI makes confident mistakes. Every fact and every question needs a teacher's check before it reaches a child.

Judgment

The teacher decides.

AI can draft a comment. Only the teacher knows the child, and the final word stays with them.

Context

Ask for India.

Generic output often misses Indian curricula, names and examples. Say your board, class and context in every prompt.

Part four

A four-week plan.

  1. Week 1

    Pick one task.

    Choose the task that takes the most time for the least value. Note how long it takes this week, without AI.

  2. Week 2

    Try it with AI.

    Use AI for that task only. Note the time, and everything you had to fix.

  3. Week 3

    Share with a colleague.

    Show one colleague what worked, what didn't, and your best prompt. Try theirs.

  4. Week 4

    Decide and reinvest.

    Keep it or drop it. If you keep it, decide where the saved time goes: one more conversation with a struggling student, one better question, one more piece of feedback.
