In July 2025, an AI system from Google DeepMind reached gold-medal standard at the International Mathematical Olympiad, confirmed by the competition's own graders. OpenAI reported the same score for its system, graded by former medallists. Both wrote their proofs in plain language, within the same two four-and-a-half-hour sessions as the human contestants. Only a few years earlier, leading AI systems struggled with school-level word problems.

An exam is a sample. In a few hours, it tries to tell us what a student has learned over a year. For a long time, recall and procedure stood in reasonably well for understanding: a student who could remember a method and apply it had usually understood something. That link is now much weaker. Recall and procedure are exactly what AI does best, and students know it.

If a machine can produce the answer, the answer alone tells us very little about the child.The starting point for rethinking assessment.

Part one

What India's own data shows.

India's largest learning survey points to the same gap. PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024 assessed over 21 lakh students in Classes 3, 6 and 9 across 781 districts. Scores fall at each stage, and fall fastest in mathematics, where applying ideas matters most.

Average scores, PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024
Class 3, language64%
Class 3, mathematics60%
Class 6, language57%
Class 6, mathematics46%
Class 9, language54%
Class 9, mathematics37%
Source: NCERT, PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024, over 21 lakh students in 74,229 schools.

Only 28% of Class 9 students could apply percentages to a problem. These students have sat many exams. The exams told us they had covered percentages. They did not tell us whether students could use them.

The system is already responding. NEP 2020 calls for assessment that tests core competencies rather than memorisation. CBSE now sets half of its Class 10 and Class 12 board papers as competency-based questions, up from 40% for Class 12 in 2023-24.

Part two

What exams capture, and what they miss.

Here is how a typical written exam compares with the things that now matter most. The right-hand column is the important one.

What we want to knowTypical exam captures itAI can now do itWorth measuring?
RecallWellYesYes, as a foundation. Little stays in a head that can't recall it.
ProcedureWellYesYes, briefly. Less weight than before.
ReasoningPartlyCan imitate itYes. Ask students to explain why, aloud.
TransferRarelyPartlyYes. Unfamiliar problems show real understanding.
JudgmentRarelyNoYes. Can they tell a right answer from a plausible wrong one?
QuestioningNeverNoYes. Can they spot what is missing?
Agency and collaborationNeverNoYes. Can they carry something through with others?

The pattern is stark: the things exams capture best are the things AI does best.

Part three

Methods that show thinking.

Schools do not need to wait for the board. A small mix of methods, used regularly, gives a much clearer picture than one big exam at the end.

MethodWhat it showsTeacher effortHolds up against AI
Two-minute oral checkReasoning, understandingLow, in rotationStrongly
Open-book, unfamiliar problemTransfer, judgmentMedium to designStrongly, if done in class
Fix the AI's wrong answerJudgment, critical readingLowStrongly
Portfolio of draftsGrowth over timeMediumWell
Project with a real audienceAgency, collaborationHigher, once a termWell
Frequent low-stakes quizRecall, progressLowIn class, yes

Part four

Measuring progress, not only position.

A single score tells you where a student stands. Progress tells you whether teaching is working. At BE Human Labs, we look at progress across our four dimensions, using measures simple enough for a busy teacher.

DimensionWhat you would seeA simple measure
AwarenessStudents can say what they understand and what they don't.Confidence rating before a quiz, compared with the score.
