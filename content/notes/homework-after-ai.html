It is nine at night. A Class 8 student opens his maths notebook, photographs the worksheet with his phone, and asks an AI assistant to solve it with steps. Fifteen minutes later, all twenty problems are done, neatly copied. His mother is pleased. His teacher will tick every answer tomorrow. Nobody in this story has learned anything about whether he understands fractions.

Homework has always had two jobs. It gives students practice, and it shows the teacher what they can do. AI has broken the second job completely, and it quietly threatens the first.

The first figure shows how quickly it has become part of daily study. The second figure is the warning. The same tool that clears a doubt can also skip the struggle that builds understanding. Homework design decides which one happens.

Part one

Catching students is the wrong game.

Many schools are responding by trying to catch AI use: stricter rules, detection software, handwritten-only policies. That road leads to suspicion, wasted teacher time and very little learning. Detection tools are unreliable, and handwriting a copied answer does not make it understood.

The question is not whether students used AI. It is whether the homework still required them to think.Redesign beats detection, every time.

Part two

What homework asks for.

Homework that AI can doHomework that needs a student
A finished product→The process: drafts, working, a voice note on how they got there
Started and finished at home→Started in class, where the teacher can see the thinking
Generic topics anyone could write→Personal: their family, street, school or experience
Marked once, then forgotten→A two-minute check the next day: "explain this part to me"
Twenty of the same problem→Five problems, one explained, and the hardest one named

Part three

A simple way to sort any task.

Two questions decide whether a homework task survives AI. Does it ask for a product or a process? Is it generic or personal? The best tasks sit in the bottom-right square.

GenericPersonal Product
AI does it

"Write an essay on pollution." Done in seconds, learned in none.

Harder to fake

"Write about pollution on your street." Better, but the thinking is still hidden.

Process
Thinking shown

"Show your working and explain step 3." Better, though steps can be copied.

Needs the student

"Measure the traffic outside your home for 10 minutes. Record a voice note on what surprised you."

Part four

Before and after, by subject.

SubjectBeforeAfter
EnglishWrite an essay on the importance of trees.Interview a grandparent about a tree they remember. Write what you learned and one question you still have.
MathsSolve exercises 1 to 20.Solve five problems. Record a one-minute voice note explaining one method. Mark the hardest and say why.
ScienceWrite notes on the water cycle.Find one place the water cycle happens in your home. Draw it and predict what changes in summer.
Social scienceAnswer the questions at the end of the chapter.Ask AI to summarise the chapter. Use your textbook to find one thing it left out or got wrong.
HindiWrite a letter to your friend about your holidays.Write a short letter to someone in your family who lives far away. Bring their reply, or their voice note, to class.

Notice what the "after" tasks share. Several use AI openly. Several involve family. All of them leave evidence of thinking that a teacher can see or ask about the next day.

Redesigning homework across a school is faster in a room together. We run a workshop where each department rewrites its homework, with examples ready to use the following week.

Part five

Less, but better.

Redesigned homework usually takes a little longer to think about and a lot less time to copy out. Many schools find they can set less of it. That is a gain, not a loss, if what remains makes students think.

It also changes what homework builds. When a task involves a grandparent, a neighbour or a family decision, it builds connection. When it asks a student to observe and act on something around them, it builds contribution. Homework becomes practice for being a Forward Thinker, not only for the next test.

Tell parents first

Parents notice when homework changes, and some will worry it is easier. A short note explaining why homework now asks for working, voice notes and family conversations turns them from worriers into partners. Our parents' note in this series can be adapted for this.
