The psychologist Susan Engel spent hours at the back of American classrooms, counting moments of curiosity: a child asking a question to learn more, or opening something up to see how it worked. In kindergarten she saw between two and five in a two-hour stretch. In Class 5, usually none to two. Those same children, at home, ask questions constantly.

Something happens between the kitchen and the classroom. Children learn that school rewards answers, that questions take time the syllabus does not have, and that asking can make you look as if you do not know. By the middle years, most of them have stopped.

That habit has become expensive. An answer can now be produced by a tool in seconds, for free. A good question still has to come from a person.

Part one

The asking gap.

If AI can answer almost anything, the difference between two students is no longer who knows more. It is who asks better. A student who asks "what is photosynthesis?" gets a definition. A student who asks "could we grow food in a room with no windows?" gets a project, a debate and a reason to learn the definition.

Questioning can be taught. Good questions have a shape, and students can learn to climb from one shape to the next.

AI can answer. The question is whether our children can ask.The theme of this year's school leadership conversations in India, and the right one.

Part two

The question ladder.

Six rungs, from remembering to acting. Every rung has value. The problem is a classroom that never climbs past the first two. The upper rungs are where a Forward Thinker lives. The top rung is where an Active Shaper begins.

Part three

How to teach it.

  1. Put the ladder on the wall, and use its names."That's a connect question. Can someone push it up to a test question?" Naming the rungs makes climbing them a habit.
  2. Start lessons with questions, not answers.Show an object, an image or a surprising fact. Students write questions before you teach anything. Then teach towards the best ones.
  3. Rate questions, not only answers.Ask the class which question would teach them the most if answered. Celebrate the asker as much as the answerer.
  4. Use AI as a sparring partner.Students ask an AI tool their question, read the answer, then write a better question the answer did not cover.
  5. Let a question drive a project.Once a term, a class chooses one question from rung four or above and investigates it properly.

The out-ask protocol

A five-minute routine for using AI to build questioning instead of replacing it.

  • Ask: students put their own question to the AI.
  • Read: they underline one thing they did not know and one thing they doubt.
  • Out-ask: they write a question one rung higher that the AI's answer did not cover.
  • Share: the best out-ask question goes on the board.
