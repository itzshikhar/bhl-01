This year, a 14-year-old in Class 9 in Lucknow will start thinking seriously about streams. Science or commerce, engineering or something else. If she follows the most common path, she will finish college around 2033 and walk into her first job. The advice she is getting is built on what first jobs looked like in 2015. The data says those jobs are already changing.

For a generation of Indian families, the logic was clear. Choose the right stream, get the right degree, and the first job would follow. That first job, often in IT services, was where people really learned their work: small tasks, close supervision, steady feedback. It was an apprenticeship with a salary.

That first rung is now thinning in some places and growing in others. Schools need to understand which, because students in Classes 9 to 12 are making choices today that will meet this market in five years.

Part one

What the data shows.

ICRIER surveyed around 650 IT companies across 10 Indian cities, looking at more than 1,900 business divisions affected by AI. The pattern is not a collapse. Senior roles were largely stable: 82% of firms reported no change. The movement was concentrated at the bottom of the ladder.

Source: ICRIER survey of around 650 IT firms in 10 cities, February 2026.

Meanwhile, the broader market for freshers is growing, but somewhere else. Naukri's February 2026 data showed fresher hiring up 17% year on year, with the strongest growth outside traditional IT. Inside IT, demand for AI and machine learning roles grew sharply.

Read together, the two sources tell a consistent story. Hiring is not disappearing. It is moving away from routine work that AI can already do, such as standard coding, testing, documentation and support, and towards roles that combine a field of knowledge with judgment and AI fluency.

Part two

What the data doesn't tell us.

We should be honest about the limits. These are early signals from one sector and one month of hiring data. We do not yet know how far the pattern spreads beyond IT, how quickly, or which new roles will grow to replace the old first rung. Anyone who claims to know exactly which careers will exist in 2033 is guessing.

The uncertainty is itself the most useful finding for a school.If no one can predict the exact jobs, the job of school is to build what transfers to any of them.

Employers seem to agree. Globally, they expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030, and the skills they rank highest are analytical thinking, resilience, leadership and creative thinking. None of these is a stream. All of them can be practised in Class 9.

Part three

What the first rung used to teach.

Entry-level work was never only about output. It was where young people learned judgment by doing small things under supervision. If AI does the small things, young people will be expected to arrive with some of that judgment already in place. Here is what that means, rung by rung.

Judgment

Is this good enough?

Used to be learned
On the job, through hundreds of small calls a manager checked.
School can start
Real projects where students make decisions and see the consequences.

Domain depth

Do I understand this field?

Used to be learned
Slowly, through routine tasks in one area.
School can start
One subject a student goes deep on, by choice, beyond the syllabus.

Working with AI

Can I direct it and check it?

Used to be learned
Not at all. It did not exist.
School can start
Using AI to extend and challenge their own thinking, with the reasoning shown.

Professional habits

Can people rely on me?

Used to be learned
From deadlines, colleagues and clients.
School can start
Responsibilities others depend on, and work for real audiences.

Part four

A plan for Classes 9 to 12.

The aim is a student who leaves school with evidence of judgment, not just marks. Each year adds one layer.

  1. Class 9

    From streams to strengths.

    Help students see what they are good at doing, not only which subjects they score well in. Short tasters across fields, followed by reflection on what energised them.

  2. Class 10

    One real problem.

    A project with a real audience, a real constraint and a real consequence: a local business, a community issue, a school system that needs fixing.

  3. Class 11

    Work alongside AI.

    Students use AI to research, draft and test ideas in their chosen field, and document how they checked and improved its output.

  4. Class 12

    A portfolio of decisions.

    A short record of what each student made, decided and learned. It goes with them into college applications and their first interviews.
